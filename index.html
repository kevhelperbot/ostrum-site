The Thesis

The Spot Market for Information

Prediction markets trade derivatives of what people know. Ostrum prices the information itself, starting where asymmetry is steepest and closest to home: market-making, exchange listing, and fundraising deals.

Information is an asset. It should have a spot market. Prediction markets turn knowledge into directional yes/no exposure; Ostrum lets participants transact in the underlying information directly.

Contribute what you know to access what others know, with exchange rates set by the value and scarcity of each data point.

Mechanism

How it Works

Ostrum runs on a strict contribute-to-unlock model. Participants add useful information and earn access to other information of comparable value.

Step 01

Contribute

Users submit information that is hard to find, costly to produce, or valuable because only a small group can observe it.

Step 02

Valuation

Contributions are valued according to their importance, the dispersion of knowledge in that segment, and the scarcity of the data point.

Step 03

Unlock & Query

Contributors query the pooled dataset through a natural-language interface, asking what others have seen in similar situations without revealing any single source.

Step 04

Verification

Submissions undergo a disclosed, time-limited review process assessed against public sources, internal data, and signs of manipulation.

Architecture

Privacy by Architecture

An information market only works if contributors can share without exposing the source. Ostrum is built around aggregation, verification, and narrow disclosure.

Coarse Bucketing

Sensitive inputs are converted to coarse buckets and ratios at submission, and raw figures are destroyed. No database of precise source material exists.

Aggregation Only

All access runs through an aggregation-only statistics layer with minimum-cohort thresholds and suppression rules. No query can return an individual submission.

Verified Contributors

Contributors are verified against their organization, so entities can only report on their own information, protecting the integrity of the market alongside privacy.
